Professional email writing tips: structure, tone and templates

Most work emails are ignored for the same few reasons: an unclear subject, the request buried in paragraph three, or a tone that is too stiff or too casual.

Quick answer

A professional email has a specific subject line, states its purpose in the first sentence, keeps to one main request, and ends with a clear next step. Polite and direct beats long and formal.

1. Write a subject line that says what you need

WeakStrong
QueryQuestion about invoice #2041
MeetingCan we move Thursday's meeting to 3pm?
Urgent!!!Approval needed by Friday: Q4 budget

2. Put the purpose first

Open with why you are writing: "I'm writing to ask whether…" or "Could you send me…". Readers decide in the first two lines whether to act now or later.

3. A simple structure

  1. Greeting — Dear Ms Khan, (formal) or Hi Ali, (familiar)
  2. Purpose — one or two sentences
  3. Details — only what the reader needs, in short paragraphs or a short list
  4. Next step — what you need, and by when
  5. Sign-off — Kind regards, or Best regards, and your name

4. Get the tone right

  • Polite but direct: Could you send the report by Thursday? rather than I was wondering if perhaps you might possibly…
  • Not too casual: avoid slang, many exclamation marks and text abbreviations
  • Not too stiff: I'll get back to you sounds more natural internationally than I'll revert

5. Following up without being annoying

Wait a reasonable time, then reply in the same thread: "Just following up on my email below — could you let me know by Friday whether…?" Short, specific and friendly.

Common mistakes

  • No subject line, or a vague one
  • The actual request hidden at the end
  • Several unrelated requests in one email
  • Replying all when only one person needs the answer
  • Attachments mentioned but not attached

Frequently asked questions

How should I start a professional email?
With a suitable greeting, then a first sentence that states your purpose, such as 'I'm writing to ask…'.

How do I end a professional email?
With a clear next step, then a sign-off such as 'Kind regards' or 'Best regards' and your name.

How long should a work email be?
As short as possible while including what the reader needs. Most good work emails fit on one screen.

Is 'Dear Sir/Madam' still used?
Only when you do not know the recipient's name. Using the person's name is better whenever you can find it.
