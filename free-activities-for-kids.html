Free activities for kids: writing, AI and coding

Seventeen free, screen-light activities parents can do at home in 20–45 minutes — each one a taste of what children explore in our workshops and courses.

No sign-up, no cost. Each activity lists the age, the time it takes, what you need and step-by-step instructions. They are grouped around our three free workshops: writing and storytelling (StoryCraft), artificial intelligence (AI Explorers) and coding (Code Quest). See all free workshops.

Story character builder: a free writing activity for kids

Every good story starts with a character readers care about. This 30-minute activity helps children invent one — and the story usually follows on its own.

Five senses setting: a descriptive writing activity for kids

Most young writers describe only what things look like. Adding sound, smell, touch and taste turns a flat setting into somewhere a reader can almost stand.

Try the activity

The 50-word story challenge: a fun writing activity for kids

A whole story — beginning, middle and end — in exactly fifty words. It sounds impossible, and that is what makes children want to try.

Try the activity

Comic strip story: a creative writing activity for kids

For children who love drawing more than writing, a comic strip is the perfect way to plan a story — and the writing becomes much easier afterwards.

Try the activity

Story dice game: make your own and tell a story

Draw pictures on paper dice, roll them, and use whatever comes up in a story. It is a game, so children forget they are practising storytelling.

Try the activity

Diary of a character: a perspective writing activity for kids

Writing as someone else is one of the best ways to understand a story — and to find a writing voice. All you need is a book your child already knows.

Try the activity

Sort it like a computer: a free AI activity for kids

Children play the role of a machine learning model with objects from around the house — and discover first-hand how AI learns, and how it gets things wrong.

Try the activity

AI or not? A home scavenger hunt for kids

AI is all around the house, hidden in apps and devices. Hunting for it helps children notice how much they already use — and start asking how it works.

Spot the AI mistake: a fact-checking activity for kids

AI can sound completely sure and still be wrong. This game teaches children the habit that matters most: check before you trust.

Decision tree guessing game: an AI and logic activity for kids

A decision tree is a chain of yes-or-no questions — the same idea behind some AI systems. Children build one that can guess an animal.

Family AI rules poster: an AI safety activity for kids

Rules children help write are rules children remember. Make a poster together that sets out how your family uses AI tools safely.

Robot sandwich: an unplugged coding activity for kids

Write instructions for making a sandwich. A parent follows them exactly, like a robot. The results are funny — and the lesson about coding sticks.

Grid maze directions: a coding activity without a computer

Draw a grid, add a start, a goal and some obstacles, then write arrow commands to get there. It is how many children first meet programming.

Loop dance routine: learn coding loops by moving

Loops are one of the most important ideas in coding. Children learn them best with their whole body — by writing and performing a dance.

Programs make decisions with "if this, then that". A deck of cards and a few rules turn that idea into a game children want to play again.

Programmers spend much of their time finding mistakes. This activity turns debugging into a detective game with a slightly silly recipe.
