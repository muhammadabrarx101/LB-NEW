AI literacy: seven skills every child needs now

Reading, writing and digital skills are no longer enough. Children now need to understand and question the AI tools they use every day.

Quick answer

AI literacy is the ability to understand what AI is, use it effectively and question what it produces. The seven skills below matter far more for children than learning to build AI.

1. Knowing what AI is — and is not

AI finds patterns in data and predicts likely answers. It does not understand, feel or know things in the way people do. Children who grasp this stop treating AI as an authority.

2. Questioning the output

Is this answer reasonable? Does it match what I already know? Where might it be wrong? Asking these questions every time is the core habit of AI literacy.

3. Checking important facts

For anything that matters — homework facts, health questions, news — children should confirm in a second, reliable source.

4. Understanding data and bias

AI learns from examples. If the examples are unbalanced, the AI will be too. Children can understand this with simple activities — see the sorting activity in our free activities.

5. Protecting privacy

Knowing what never to share — names, school, address, photos, passwords — and that what you type may be stored.

6. Asking good questions (prompting)

Clear, specific questions get better answers. "Explain fractions using pizza, for a 10-year-old" works far better than "fractions". Prompting well is really just communicating clearly.

7. Using AI honestly

Knowing when AI help is allowed, crediting it, and keeping the thinking your own. See how kids should use AI for homework.

How to build these skills at home

  • Use AI together and talk about the answers
  • Look for mistakes on purpose — finding one is a great lesson
  • Ask "how do you think it knew that?"
  • Agree family rules for AI use

Frequently asked questions

What is AI literacy?
The ability to understand what AI is, use it effectively and responsibly, and question what it produces.

Why do children need AI literacy?
Because they already use AI tools daily, and need to know when to trust them, when to check them and how to stay safe.

Can young children learn AI literacy?
Yes. Children from about 7 can learn that AI learns from examples and can make mistakes, through simple games and activities.

Is AI literacy the same as coding?
No. AI literacy is about understanding and using AI wisely; coding is about writing programs. Both are useful.
