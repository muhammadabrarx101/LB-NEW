How to get band 7 in IELTS: a study plan that works

Band 7 is the target for most universities and many migration routes. Here is what it actually requires in each module, and a weekly plan to get there.

Quick answer

To get band 7, you typically need around 30 out of 40 in Listening, around 30 out of 40 in Academic Reading (about 34 in General Training), and Writing and Speaking that are well organised, mostly accurate and use a good range of vocabulary. Most students move from 6 to 7 by fixing a few specific weaknesses, not by doing more of everything.

What band 7 looks like in each module

ModuleBand 7 in practice
ListeningAbout 30 of 40 correct; spelling and plurals right
Reading (Academic)About 30 of 40; fast scanning and precise True/False/Not Given
WritingClear position, logical paragraphs, a range of structures with errors that do not block meaning
SpeakingSpeaks at length without effort, uses some less common vocabulary, mostly accurate

Raw-score conversions vary slightly between tests. For the full picture, read IELTS band scores explained.

The skills that move a 6 to a 7

Writing is where most candidates get stuck. The common gaps:

  • Task response: answering a slightly different question from the one asked
  • Paragraph logic: one clear idea per paragraph, developed with an example
  • Accuracy: a few error-free complex sentences beat many risky ones

Speaking needs fluency more than fancy words. Band 7 speakers keep going without long pauses, extend their answers with reasons and examples, and self-correct naturally.

Reading is mostly about time. Students who read every word run out of time; band 7 readers skim for structure, then scan for answers.

Listening marks are often lost on spelling, plurals and missed words in distractors, not on understanding.

An 8-week plan to reach band 7

WeekFocus
1Full timed practice test to find your real band in each module
2Writing Task 2 structure; Listening sections 1 and 2
3Reading: True/False/Not Given and matching headings; Speaking Part 1 fluency
4Writing Task 1 (Academic charts or GT letters); Listening sections 3 and 4
5Speaking Part 2 cue cards daily; Reading timing drills
6Two Task 2 essays a week with feedback; vocabulary by topic
7Second full practice test; fix the weakest module
8Timed mixed practice, rest, and exam-day routine

Plan for 60 to 90 minutes a day. If your starting band is 5.5 or below, allow more time: see how long IELTS preparation actually takes.

Daily habits that help

  • Write one paragraph a day and check it against the band descriptors
  • Answer one Speaking question aloud and record yourself
  • Read one academic article and summarise it in three sentences
  • Keep a mistakes notebook for Listening and Reading answers you got wrong, and why

Frequently asked questions

How many correct answers do I need for band 7 in IELTS Listening?
Usually around 30 out of 40. The exact conversion can vary slightly between test versions.

Is band 7 in IELTS hard to get?
It is achievable for most students with a solid B2 level of English and focused preparation. The biggest jump is usually in Writing.

How long does it take to go from band 6 to band 7?
Often 6 to 10 weeks of focused practice, depending on which modules are weakest and how much time you can give each day.

Can I prepare for IELTS band 7 at home?
Yes, with practice tests and a plan. The hardest part to do alone is Writing and Speaking feedback, which is where a teacher helps most.

Which IELTS module is hardest for band 7?
For most candidates, Writing. Clear task response, paragraph logic and accuracy usually make the difference.
