A Level English Literature tuition online

Live online A Level English Literature classes for Cambridge 9695: close reading of set texts, unseen analysis, and essays with a clear argument.

Students who love reading are not automatically good at A Level English Literature. The exam rewards a clear argument about a text, supported by close analysis of language, form and structure, and written under time pressure. We teach students how to read closely and how to turn that reading into essays that examiners reward.

  • Poetry, prose and drama set texts, including Shakespeare, chosen by your school from the Cambridge list
  • Unseen critical analysis of a passage or poem you have not studied

Because set texts change by exam series, tell us which texts your child is studying and we will plan the sessions around them.

How we teach A Level English Literature

Every student starts with a topic-by-topic diagnostic. "Weak at English Literature" is not specific enough to fix: we need to know which topics lose marks, and whether the problem is understanding, recall or exam technique.

  1. Diagnose: a short test across the specification, marked against real Cambridge mark schemes.
  2. Rebuild concepts: we fix the underlying idea first, because technique built on a shaky concept collapses under exam pressure.
  3. Past-paper cycles: topic-wise questions first, then full papers, learning the command words and exactly what examiners credit.
  4. Timed practice and tracking: full papers under exam timing in the final weeks, with progress tracked topic by topic.

Classes are live online in groups of 3 to 5 students, or one-to-one when a student is starting late, retaking or has very specific gaps. Sessions are recorded for revision, and parents get honest progress updates on WhatsApp. We teach students across Pakistan (Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad and beyond), the Gulf, the UK and North America.

Where students usually lose marks

  • Retelling the story instead of analysing how the writer creates meaning
  • Quotations that are too long, or not analysed at word level
  • No clear argument: essays that list points without a thesis
  • Unseen texts approached without a method
  • Timing, especially when two essays must be written in one paper

Frequently asked questions

Do you teach the exact set texts my child is studying?
Yes. Tell us the texts and the exam series and we plan the sessions around them.

Can you help with unseen poetry and prose?
Yes. We teach a repeatable method for unseen analysis and practise it on past-paper passages.

Will this help with university applications in English?
Strong close reading and essay writing help with English, law and humanities applications, as well as the exam itself.

Is one-to-one available for English Literature?
Yes. Many Literature students prefer one-to-one because essay feedback is so individual. Small groups are also available.
