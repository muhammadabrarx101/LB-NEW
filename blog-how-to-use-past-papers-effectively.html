How to use past papers effectively for IGCSE and O Level

Doing past papers is not the same as learning from them. The marking and the follow-up are where the grade actually improves.

Quick answer

Start with topic-by-topic questions, mark every answer against the official mark scheme, record lost marks in an error log, re-attempt only what you got wrong, and move to full timed papers in the final months.

Stage 1: topic questions

Early in the exam year, use past-paper questions by topic as you finish each one in class. This shows you how that topic is actually examined.

Stage 2: mark properly

Mark your answer against the mark scheme, not against what you think is right. Notice:

  • The exact keywords that earn marks
  • How marks are split across a longer answer
  • What examiners accept, and what they do not

After a few weeks, patterns appear. Most students lose marks in a handful of topics or habits: fixing those is the fastest way up.

Stage 4: re-attempt the wrong ones

Redo only the questions you got wrong, a week or two later, without looking at the answer. If you get them right now, the learning has stuck.

Stage 5: full timed papers

In the final two or three months, sit complete papers under exam timing. This trains pace, stamina and decision-making, when to move on, how much to write.

Common mistakes

  • Doing papers but never marking them properly
  • Looking at the mark scheme before trying
  • Only doing papers you find easy
  • Using papers from the wrong syllabus or an old specification

Check the syllabus code

Make sure papers match your syllabus code and the current specification, as content and paper structures change over time.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start doing past papers?
Use topic questions as you finish each topic, then full timed papers in the final two or three months.

How many past papers should I do?
Quality matters more than number. A paper you mark carefully and learn from is worth more than several you rush.

Should I look at the mark scheme first?
No. Attempt the question first, then mark it against the mark scheme.

What is an error log?
A record of every lost mark, the question, topic, reason and fix, which shows the patterns you need to work on.
