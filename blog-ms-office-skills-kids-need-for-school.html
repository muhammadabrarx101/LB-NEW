The MS Office skills kids need for school: Word, PowerPoint and Excel

Most school projects quietly depend on Office skills that nobody formally teaches. A few core skills save hours and make work look far more professional.

Quick answer

The essentials are: in Word, headings, formatting and inserting images properly; in PowerPoint, clear slides with little text; in Excel, entering data, simple formulas and charts. These cover most school projects from about age 10.

Word: the skills that matter

  • Using heading styles instead of just making text bigger
  • Formatting — fonts, spacing and alignment that stay consistent
  • Inserting and positioning images without breaking the layout
  • Tables for organised information
  • Spell check — and knowing it does not catch everything
  • Saving and naming files sensibly

PowerPoint: clearer slides

  • One idea per slide
  • Few words — slides support what you say, they are not a script
  • Consistent design — the same fonts and colours throughout
  • Images that add meaning, not decoration
  • Rehearsing with the slides

Excel: the basics

  • Entering data in rows and columns
  • Simple formulas — SUM, AVERAGE, adding and multiplying cells
  • Sorting and filtering
  • Making a chart from data
  • Formatting numbers, dates and percentages

Common mistakes to fix early

  • Spacing with lots of spaces or returns instead of proper formatting
  • Ten fonts in one document
  • Slides covered in paragraphs of text
  • Typing totals by hand in Excel instead of using formulas
  • Losing work because files were never saved properly

Why these skills matter

Office skills are used across school subjects, in university and in almost every job. Children who learn them properly early spend less time fighting the software and more time on the actual work.

Frequently asked questions

What computer skills do kids need for school?
Core Word, PowerPoint and Excel skills: formatting documents, making clear slides, and entering data with simple formulas and charts.

What age should kids learn Microsoft Office?
From around 10, when school projects start needing documents, presentations and simple data work.

Is Excel too hard for children?
Not the basics. Entering data, simple formulas like SUM and making charts are very learnable from about 10.

How do I help my child make better presentations?
One idea per slide, few words, consistent design and practising the talk with the slides.
