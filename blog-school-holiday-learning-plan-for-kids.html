A school holiday learning plan for kids — without ruining the break

Holidays are for rest. A light routine, one creative project and a short course can stop learning loss without turning the break into more school.

Quick answer

Keep it light and balanced: a short daily reading habit, a little maths or writing practice, one creative project the child chooses, and perhaps one short course or workshop. Protect plenty of free time, outdoor play and sleep.

Why bother?

Long breaks can cause some learning loss, especially in maths and reading. A small amount of regular practice prevents most of it — and a creative project can be the highlight of the holiday.

A sample weekly rhythm

DayLight learning (30–45 min)
MonReading + a few maths puzzles
TueCreative project time
WedReading + a short writing activity
ThuCreative project time
FriReading + a fun quiz or game
WeekendFree time, family time, outings

Choose one creative project

Let your child pick something they would genuinely enjoy finishing by the end of the holiday:

  • Write and illustrate a short story or comic
  • Design a simple video game — on paper or in code
  • Run a small "business" selling crafts to family
  • A science experiment journal

Try our free activities for kids for ideas.

Consider one short course or workshop

A short, live course adds structure and a new skill without taking over the holiday. Good holiday picks include coding, creative writing, art and science clubs — things school does not always cover. Our free workshops are a no-commitment way to start.

What to avoid

  • Hours of worksheets every day
  • Packing every day with classes
  • Holiday homework that causes daily battles
  • Letting screens fill all the free time

Protect rest

Children come back to school sharper after a genuine break. Sleep, outdoor play and boredom (which sparks creativity) are part of a good holiday plan.

Frequently asked questions

How much should kids study during holidays?
A light 30 to 45 minutes on most weekdays is enough to prevent learning loss while protecting the break.

What are good holiday activities for kids?
Reading, a creative project they choose, outdoor play and perhaps one short course or workshop in something new.

Do children forget what they learned over holidays?
Some learning loss is common, especially in maths and reading, but a small amount of regular practice prevents most of it.

Are online courses good for holidays?
Short, live, project-based courses can add structure and a new skill without taking over the break.
