Advantages and disadvantages of online learning for kids and students

Online classes are now a normal part of school, tuition and exam preparation. Here is an honest look at what works, what does not, and how to get the benefits without the downsides.

Quick answer

The main advantages of online learning are flexibility, no travel, access to specialist teachers anywhere, lower overall cost and recorded lessons for revision. The main disadvantages are more screen time, harder focus for younger children, less face-to-face social contact and dependence on a good internet connection. Most disadvantages shrink when classes are live, small-group and interactive rather than pre-recorded videos.

The advantages of online learning

1. Flexibility and time saved

Online classes fit around school, sport and family. A one-hour tuition class that once took two hours with travel now takes one. For exam students in IGCSE, A-Level, IELTS or SAT preparation, that time goes back into revision.

2. Access to the right teacher, wherever they are

A family is no longer limited to tutors within driving distance. A student in a small town, or abroad, can learn from a specialist in their exact subject or exam board.

3. Recordings and resources for revision

When live lessons are recorded, students can rewatch a difficult explanation before a test. Shared notes, slides and past papers stay in one place.

4. Lower overall cost

Online learning removes travel costs and the overheads of a physical centre. For families, the real saving is often time as much as money.

5. Confidence for quieter students

Many shy children speak up more in a small online group, using the chat, a shared whiteboard or their microphone, than in a crowded classroom.

6. Modern skills come naturally

Learning online builds digital skills — typing, file sharing, online research and safe use of tools — that students need anyway. For subjects like coding for kids, Python and AI for kids, the computer is the classroom.

The disadvantages of online learning

1. Screen time

Online classes add to the hours a child spends in front of a screen. What matters most is how the time is used: creative screen time, where a child writes, builds or solves, is very different from passive watching.

2. Focus and motivation, especially for younger children

Children under about ten can drift off in long or passive sessions. Recorded video courses are the hardest; live classes with a teacher who calls on each child work far better.

3. Less face-to-face contact

Online groups lack break-time chat and in-person friendships. Small groups where students talk to each other, not just to the teacher, close part of that gap.

4. Internet and device problems

A weak connection or a shared device can disrupt a lesson. A simple computer setup for online classes prevents most problems.

5. Quality varies

"Online course" can mean anything from a live expert teacher to a pre-recorded video with no feedback. Choosing well matters more than whether learning is online or in person.

Online learning for kids

For children aged about 6 to 12, online learning works best when:

  • Sessions are short and live, around 45–60 minutes
  • Groups are small, so every child speaks
  • Children make something — a story, a game, a project — rather than only watch
  • A parent is nearby for the first few sessions

Courses like creative writing for kids, Math Magic and coding for kids suit this format well.

Online learning for exam students

For older students preparing for IGCSE, O Level, A-Level, IELTS or the SAT, online learning has extra advantages:

  • Past papers and mark schemes shared on screen and marked in the lesson
  • Timed practice and mock tests at home
  • Specialist teachers for each subject or test

See IGCSE tuition, A-Level tuition, IELTS preparation and SAT preparation.

How to get the advantages without the disadvantages

  1. Choose live, small-group classes over pre-recorded videos.
  2. Set up a quiet spot with a reliable device and headphones.
  3. Keep sessions focused — shorter and more frequent beats long and rare.
  4. Ask for recordings and use them before tests.
  5. Check the teacher first with a free trial or free demo class.
  6. Balance screen time with reading, outdoor play and offline practice.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main advantages of online learning?
Flexibility, no travel, access to specialist teachers anywhere, recorded lessons for revision and often a lower overall cost.

What are the main disadvantages of online learning?
More screen time, harder focus for younger children, less face-to-face social contact, and dependence on a stable internet connection and device.

Is online learning better than face-to-face learning?
For most academic subjects and skills courses, live online classes in small groups give results similar to face-to-face classes. Face-to-face still suits very young children and hands-on practical work better.

Is online learning good for kids?
Yes, when classes are live, short, interactive and in small groups. Pre-recorded videos with no teacher contact work far less well for children.

How can students overcome the disadvantages of online learning?
Choose live small-group classes, set up a quiet study space, keep sessions short, use recordings for revision and balance screen time with offline activities.
