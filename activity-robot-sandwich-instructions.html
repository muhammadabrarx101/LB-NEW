Robot sandwich: an unplugged coding activity for kids

Write instructions for making a sandwich. A parent follows them exactly, like a robot. The results are funny — and the lesson about coding sticks.

Computers do exactly what they are told — not what we meant. This activity teaches that lesson in the most memorable way possible, with no screens at all.

  • Ages: 6–12
  • Time: 20 minutes
  • Skill: Writing precise algorithms

What you need

  • Bread, a spread and a knife (or pretend items)
  • Paper and a pencil
  • A parent willing to be a robot

Step by step

  1. Explain the job. Your child is the programmer. The parent is a robot that does exactly what it is told — nothing more.
  2. Write the instructions. Your child writes numbered steps for making a sandwich.
  3. Run the program. The robot follows each step literally. "Put butter on the bread" — the robot puts the whole tub on top of the loaf.
  4. Find the bug. Stop when something goes wrong. Which step was unclear?
  5. Fix and re-run. Rewrite the step to be more precise, and try again.
  6. Celebrate. Keep going until the robot makes a sandwich successfully.

What your child learns

An algorithm is a precise set of steps. A bug is a mistake in the steps. Debugging is finding and fixing it. These are the core ideas of all programming.

Make it easier or harder

  • Easier: make a glass of water instead.
  • Harder: add a condition: "If there is no butter, use jam."

Frequently asked questions

What is an unplugged coding activity?
An activity that teaches coding ideas, such as algorithms and debugging, without using a computer.

What age is the robot sandwich activity for?
About 6 to 12. Younger children can say the steps aloud while an adult writes them.
