How to help your child with homework — without doing it for them

The aim of homework is your child’s learning, not a perfect page. These habits help without turning you into the one doing the work.

Quick answer

Set up a routine and a quiet space, ask questions instead of giving answers, let your child struggle a little before helping, and talk to the teacher if homework regularly causes tears or takes far too long.

Set up the basics

  • A regular time, ideally after a short break and a snack
  • A quiet space with the materials ready
  • Phones away — for the child and, ideally, the parent nearby

Routine removes the daily argument about when homework happens.

Ask, do not tell

When your child is stuck, try questions before answers:

  • What is the question asking you to do?
  • What do you already know about this?
  • Can you show me a similar example from class?
  • What could you try first?

These questions teach the process of getting unstuck — which is the real skill.

Let them struggle a little

A few minutes of productive struggle is where learning happens. Jumping in too quickly teaches children to wait for rescue. If they are still stuck after a genuine try, give a hint, not the answer.

Praise effort and strategy

"You kept trying different ways until it worked" builds persistence. "You're so clever" can make children avoid hard work in case they fail.

Know when to step back

If you find yourself writing sentences, solving the problems or redoing work so it looks neat, step back. Teachers need to see what your child can actually do.

When to talk to the teacher

  • Homework regularly takes far longer than intended
  • Your child is often upset or anxious about it
  • They do not understand the topic at all, repeatedly

That is useful information for the teacher, not a failure.

Frequently asked questions

Should I help my child with homework?
Yes, by setting up a routine, asking guiding questions and giving hints — but not by doing the work for them.

How long should homework take?
It depends on age and school. If it regularly takes far longer than expected, talk to the teacher.

What do I do when my child refuses homework?
Keep a consistent routine, break the work into small parts, and look for the reason — it is often that the work feels too hard or unclear.

Should I correct my child's homework?
Point out where to look again, but let them make the corrections. Teachers need to see their real work.
