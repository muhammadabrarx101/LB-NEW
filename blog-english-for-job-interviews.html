English for job interviews: answers, phrases and practice tips

Interviewers are not grading your grammar. They are listening for clear, structured answers with real examples, and that is a skill you can prepare.

Quick answer

Prepare structured answers with real examples, using the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Practise them aloud until they sound natural rather than memorised. Clear and confident matters far more than perfect grammar.

The STAR method

For any question that begins "Tell me about a time when…":

StepWhat to sayExample
SituationBrief background"In my last role, our team missed two deadlines in a row."
TaskYour responsibility"I was asked to find out why."
ActionWhat you did"I mapped the process and found a two-day approval delay, so I proposed a simpler sign-off."
ResultThe outcome, ideally measurable"We delivered the next three projects on time."

Common questions to prepare

  • Tell me about yourself. (A two-minute summary: background, key strengths, why this role)
  • Why do you want this job?
  • What are your strengths and weaknesses?
  • Tell me about a challenge you faced.
  • Where do you see yourself in five years?
  • Do you have any questions for us?: always have one or two

Useful phrases

  • Buying time: "That's a good question: let me think for a moment."
  • Clarifying: "Just to make sure I understand, are you asking about…?"
  • Giving examples: "For example…" / "A good example of that was when…"
  • Talking about weaknesses positively: "Something I've been working on is…"
  • Closing: "Thank you for your time: I'm very interested in the role."

Mistakes to avoid

  • Memorised answers delivered word for word
  • Very short answers with no examples
  • Very long answers with no clear point
  • Speaking too fast because of nerves

How to practise

Record yourself answering the common questions, listen back and shorten anything over two minutes. Then practise with someone who can ask follow-up questions. That is where real interviews differ from rehearsal.

Frequently asked questions

How can I answer interview questions confidently in English?
Prepare structured answers with real examples using the STAR method, then practise them aloud until they feel natural.

What is the STAR method?
A way to structure answers: Situation, Task, Action and Result. It keeps answers clear and focused on what you did.

Will grammar mistakes cost me the job?
Small mistakes rarely matter. Interviewers focus on clear communication, relevant examples and confidence.

How should I answer 'Tell me about yourself'?
Give a short summary of your background, two or three key strengths, and why you want this role: in about two minutes.
