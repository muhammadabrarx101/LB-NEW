Five senses setting: a descriptive writing activity for kids

Most young writers describe only what things look like. Adding sound, smell, touch and taste turns a flat setting into somewhere a reader can almost stand.

A strong setting makes a story feel real. The trick children are rarely taught is simple: use more than your eyes. This activity takes about 25 minutes and works for any place your child knows or imagines.

  • Ages: 7–12
  • Time: 25 minutes
  • Skill: Descriptive writing

What you need

  • Paper or a notebook
  • A pencil
  • Optional: a photo of an interesting place

Step by step

  1. Choose a place. A kitchen at dinner time, a busy market, a beach, a forest at night, or a place from a photo.
  2. Make five columns. Label them See, Hear, Smell, Touch and Taste.
  3. Fill each column. Write at least two details in each. Encourage specific details: not "food" but "hot parathas".
  4. Pick the best five. Circle the five most interesting details, one from each sense if possible.
  5. Write one paragraph. Turn the five details into a short paragraph describing the place, without saying "I can see" every time.
  6. Read it aloud. Ask: could someone picture this place without being told its name?

Make it easier or harder

  • Easier: sit somewhere real, the kitchen, the garden, and describe it as it is.
  • Harder: describe the same place at two different times, such as a school in the morning and at midnight.

What your child learns

Sensory detail is one of the fastest ways to improve school compositions, and examiners reward it at every level.
