Digital safety for kids: passwords, privacy, scams and AI

Children are online earlier than ever. A few clear habits protect them from most everyday risks, and they are easier to teach than many parents expect.

Quick answer

The core habits are: strong, private passwords; sharing as little personal information as possible; checking before clicking; being careful with online friends; and telling a trusted adult when something feels wrong.

Passwords

  • Use long passphrases: several random words together are stronger than short complex ones
  • Different passwords for important accounts
  • Never share passwords with friends
  • Turn on two-step verification where available

Personal information

Teach children what never to post or share with strangers or AI tools:

  • Full name, school, home address, phone number
  • Photos in school uniform or showing the house number
  • Live location
  • Passwords and verification codes

Spotting scams and fake messages

Warning signs include:

  • Urgency: "act now or lose your account"
  • Prizes you did not enter for
  • Requests for codes or passwords
  • Links that look slightly wrong
  • Messages from "friends" asking for money or codes

Rule of thumb: stop, check with an adult, then decide.

Online friends

People online are not always who they say they are. Children should not share personal details, move to private chats with strangers, or agree to meet someone they only know online. They should always tell a trusted adult if someone makes them uncomfortable.

AI tools

Treat AI chatbots like any other online service: no personal details, check important facts, and remember it is software, not a friend. See is AI safe for kids?.

For parents

  • Keep devices in shared spaces for younger children
  • Use age-appropriate settings and parental controls
  • Have regular, calm conversations: children share more when they do not fear losing their device
  • Model the habits yourself

Frequently asked questions

How do I teach my child about online safety?
Through regular, calm conversations and a few clear habits: private passwords, no personal details, check before clicking and tell an adult if something feels wrong.

What personal information should kids never share online?
Full name, school, home address, phone number, live location, passwords and verification codes.

How can children spot online scams?
Look for urgency, unexpected prizes, requests for codes or passwords and slightly wrong links, then stop and check with an adult.

Should I use parental controls?
Age-appropriate controls help, especially for younger children, alongside open conversations and shared spaces for devices.
