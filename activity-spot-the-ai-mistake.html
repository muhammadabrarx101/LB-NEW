Spot the AI mistake: a fact-checking activity for kids

AI can sound completely sure and still be wrong. This game teaches children the habit that matters most: check before you trust.

One of the most important things children can learn about AI is that sounding confident is not the same as being right. This game builds that habit without needing a computer, or, for older children, with a supervised AI tool.

  • Ages: 8–14
  • Time: 20 minutes
  • Skill: Questioning and checking information

What you need

  • Paper and a pencil
  • A children’s encyclopedia, textbook or a trusted website for checking
  • Optional: a parent-supervised AI tool

Step by step

  1. Prepare "AI answers". The parent writes five confident answers to questions, with two containing a deliberate mistake (for example, "Spiders are insects with six legs").
  2. Read them like an AI. Read each answer in a confident, robot-like voice.
  3. Child judges each one. For each answer: true, false or not sure?
  4. Check the facts. Look up the doubtful ones in a book or trusted site.
  5. Reveal the mistakes. Discuss how confident the wrong answers sounded.
  6. Swap roles. Now the child writes "AI answers" with hidden mistakes for the parent.

Using a real AI tool (older children, supervised)

With a parent, ask an AI tool a few questions about a topic your child knows well. Check its answers against a book. Finding even one mistake is a powerful lesson.

What your child learns

Healthy scepticism, fact-checking and the understanding that AI predicts likely answers. It does not know the truth. Read is AI safe for kids? and AI literacy skills every child needs.
