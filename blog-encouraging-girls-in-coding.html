Encouraging girls in coding: a practical guide for parents

Girls are just as capable at coding as boys. What changes outcomes is early exposure, confidence and seeing that technology is creative and for them.

Quick answer

Girls are equally capable of coding. The biggest factors are early, positive exposure, creative projects, role models and confidence when things go wrong. Parents can influence all four.

Start early and keep it fun

Interest in technology often forms before the teenage years. Simple coding games, animations and design projects at 8–12 build familiarity before stereotypes take hold.

Lead with creativity

Coding is creative. Projects like animated stories, games about their interests, digital art and designs often appeal more than abstract exercises. When girls see that code is a way to make things, interest follows.

Show role models

Talk about women in technology, science and engineering — historical figures like Ada Lovelace, as well as women working in technology today. Seeing that people like them do this work matters.

Make mistakes normal

Many capable students give up after early errors because they assume they are "not good at it". Explain that every programmer debugs constantly — mistakes are part of the process, not a judgement.

Small groups help

In a small group, every student speaks and gets help quickly. That makes it easier for quieter students to ask questions and build confidence.

Watch your language

Avoid describing tech as "for boys" or praising one child as "naturally techy". Praise effort and problem-solving instead: "You kept going until you fixed that bug."

  4. Celebrate what she builds

Frequently asked questions

Are girls good at coding?
Yes. Girls are just as capable at coding as boys. Exposure, confidence and encouragement make the difference.

How can I get my daughter interested in coding?
Start with creative projects linked to her interests, show role models, make mistakes feel normal and celebrate what she builds.

What age should girls start coding?
The same as boys — simple, playful coding from around 7 to 10, with more structured courses from about 10.

Do small classes help girls in coding?
Small groups help every student speak up and get help quickly, which builds confidence for quieter learners.
